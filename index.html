1. Summary

  • The games can be played in full without an account, without signing up and offline. Your progress is stored on your device.
  • They show ads through Google AdMob, which may also pick ads from the Unity Ads and Liftoff networks: a banner at the bottom of the screen, full-screen ads between levels, and rewarded ads that you choose to watch in exchange for an extra hint.
  • When you are online, your best scores go into a global ranking under a random code that does not reveal who you are. In the leaderboard you appear as “Player” followed by four characters or, if you are signed in to Google Play Games, with your player name.
  • There are no in-app purchases or analytics tools, and the developer does not sell personal data.

2. Data processed

2.1 Progress and settings (on your device)

Each game stores in its private storage, on your device, what it needs to remember your progress: the levels you have completed with their stars and scores, the hints and chests you have earned, the days you have played the daily challenge and your streak, and your settings (music, sound, vibration and reduce motion). It also stores the random ranking code there (section 2.3).

  • This information does not contain your real name, email address or phone number.
  • It is deleted when you uninstall the game or clear its data in Android settings.
  • If Android backup is turned on, the system may include it in your Google account backup and restore it when you reinstall the game or switch phones. You control this in Android’s backup settings.

The daily challenge level is chosen from the date, on the device itself.

2.2 Advertising (Google AdMob, Unity Ads and Liftoff)

The games show three kinds of ads through Google AdMob:

  • Banner: a fixed strip at the bottom of the screen, both in the menus and while you play.
  • Full-screen ads: at most one every ten levels won, and when you lose all your lives; never two within a minute.
  • Rewarded ads: only when you tap the button to watch one in exchange for an extra hint. If no ad is available, you get the hint anyway.

For each ad, AdMob chooses between Google’s advertising and that of two partner ad networks (mediation): Unity Ads (Unity Technologies) and Liftoff Monetize (Liftoff Mobile, Inc.). Many of their full-screen ads are interactive: short demos of other games that you can try for a few seconds before closing them.

The ad components included in the games (AdMob’s, and those of Unity Ads and Liftoff) connect to their servers to prepare and show ads, and may process the following data:

DataPurpose
Your device’s advertising ID and Android’s app set ID Ad selection and measurement, frequency capping and fraud prevention
Technical device data (model, operating system, language, IP address and the approximate location derived from it) Ad compatibility, tailoring ads to your region, and security
Your interaction with ads (impressions, taps, completed views) and diagnostic data from the ad component Measurement, billing and bug fixing

Unity and Liftoff process this data as independent controllers, under their own policies: the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy and the Liftoff Privacy Policy.

The games only request ads suitable for teens (Google’s “T” content rating).

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the games ask for your consent through Google’s form (User Messaging Platform) before loading ads. Unity and Liftoff are listed in it among the ad partners, and your choice also applies to them. You can review or change it at any time from Settings > Privacy, on the home screen or in the game’s pause menu. The legal bases are:

  • Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR) for personalised ads and for accessing information on your device, such as the advertising ID.
  • The legitimate interest of Google, Unity and Liftoff (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) for other purposes shown in the form, such as showing non-personalised ads, measuring their performance and preventing fraud. You can object in the same form, under “Manage options”.

If you do not give your consent, any ads shown will not be personalised, or no ads will be shown. You can read how Google uses advertising data at policies.google.com/technologies/ads.

In addition, you can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in Android settings (Privacy > Ads).

2.3 Global ranking (Supabase)

Each game has a global leaderboard with every player’s position in each level and in the sum of all levels. It is stored in a database belonging to the developer, hosted in London (United Kingdom) by Supabase (Supabase, Inc.), which acts as a data processor. When you win a level or open the leaderboard while online, the game sends:

DataPurpose
A random code that the game creates the first time it needs it. It is not derived from your data or your device’s, it is never shown, and the server only stores a fingerprint (hash) of it Telling your scores apart from other players’. Four characters of that fingerprint make up your “Player XXXX” label
The level, your best score in it and the date Working out your position in each level and in the overall leaderboard
Your Google Play Games player name, only if you are signed in (section 2.4) Showing you with that name in the leaderboard

Your name or label and your total points are visible to any other player in the leaderboard. No email address, password or real name is stored, and the games never ask you to type anything. If you play offline nothing is sent, and the game works the same without the ranking.

Like any internet service, the servers keep a technical, temporary record of the IP address the game connects from, for security. The legal basis for this processing is the legitimate interest in offering a leaderboard between players (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR), using minimal data that does not identify you directly.

To stay within the database’s free plan, if it gets close to its limit the ranking becomes weekly: each week starts from scratch and scores from previous weeks are deleted automatically.

2.4 Google Play Games

If your device has a Google Play Games profile, the game may sign in with it when it opens. While signed in:

  • the game receives your Play Games player name from Google (not your email address or real name) and uses it in the ranking (section 2.3);
  • your progress (section 2.1) is saved in Saved Games, in your Google account, so that you can recover it on another device.

This processing is carried out by Google under the Google Privacy Policy. You can turn off automatic sign-in, unlink the game from your account or delete your saved data in the Google Play Games settings. Without signing in, the game is just as playable and nothing in this section applies.

2.5 Distribution through Google Play

Downloads, installation and, where applicable, participation in testing programmes are handled by Google Play under its own policies. See the Google Privacy Policy.

2.6 System crash reports

If you have turned on sending usage and diagnostics data in Android settings, the system may send Google reports of the games’ crashes and errors. The developer only sees them aggregated and anonymised in the Google Play console, with no way to identify anyone. The games do not include any analytics or crash-reporting tool of their own.

3. Who the data is shared with

The developer does not sell, rent or hand over personal data to third parties. The data described is only processed by:

  • Google (AdMob, Google Play Games and Google Play), as controller or processor depending on the case, under the policies linked in each section;
  • Unity Technologies (Unity Ads) and Liftoff Mobile, Inc. (Liftoff Monetize), ad networks that may serve the ads through AdMob, as independent controllers under their own policies (section 2.2);
  • Supabase, as the processor that hosts the ranking (section 2.3);
  • other players, who see your name or label and your points in the leaderboard.

4. International transfers

The ranking database is in the United Kingdom, which has an adequacy decision from the European Commission. Google, Unity, Liftoff and Supabase may process data on servers outside the European Economic Area, for example in the United States, with the safeguards of European Commission adequacy decisions or standard contractual clauses.

5. Retention

  • Progress and settings stay on your device while the game is installed and are deleted when you uninstall it. Their copies in your Google account (Saved Games or Android backup) remain until you delete them.
  • Ranking scores are kept while the leaderboard is active or until you ask for them to be deleted (section 7). In weekly mode, scores from previous weeks are deleted automatically.
  • Data processed by Google, Unity and Liftoff is kept for the periods set out in their policies.

6. Children

The games are intended for people aged 13 and over and do not knowingly collect data from children under that age. If you find that a child under 13 has provided personal data through them, write to the contact address to have it deleted.

7. Your rights and how to delete your data

You can exercise your rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and portability, and withdraw your consent at any time:

  • Advertising: review or withdraw your consent, or object to legitimate interest, in Settings > Privacy within the game, and reset or delete your advertising ID in Android settings. Your choice also applies to Unity and Liftoff, with whom you can also exercise your rights directly (section 2.2).
  • Ranking: write to sakepalace.apps@gmail.com stating the game, the name or label you appear with in the leaderboard and your approximate total points, and your scores will be deleted within 30 days at most. Since the ranking code is never shown anywhere, those details are what allow your scores to be found. You do not need to keep the game installed. If you do not want to appear in the ranking, play offline.
  • Cloud progress and sign-in: delete or unlink them in the Google Play Games settings.
  • Device data: delete it by clearing the game’s data or uninstalling it.

If you believe the processing does not comply with the law, you can lodge a complaint with the Spanish Data Protection Agency (www.aepd.es) or with the supervisory authority of your country of residence.

8. California residents

If you live in California, the CCPA gives you the right to know what personal information is collected, to ask for it to be deleted or corrected, and not to be discriminated against for exercising those rights. The developer does not sell personal information for money, but the use of advertising data (such as the advertising ID) by Google AdMob, Unity Ads and Liftoff may be considered “selling” or “sharing” for advertising purposes under the CCPA. You can prevent it by deleting your advertising ID in Android settings or, when the game shows that option in your region, from Settings > Privacy, which also passes your choice on to Unity and Liftoff. For your other rights, write to the contact address.

9. Security

Game data lives in the private storage that Android reserves for each app, isolated from other apps. Communications with the ranking, with Google’s services and with the ad networks are encrypted (HTTPS). The ranking database does not allow direct reading or writing: the games can only save their own score and look up the leaderboard, and cannot change other players’ scores. No email address, password or real name is stored in it.

10. Changes to this policy

Any change will be published at this same address, with the date at the top updated. If a change affects the data collected, it will be noted in the release notes and, where the law requires it, your consent will be requested again.
